Report of visit by WCCL Grounds and Facilities Subcommittee - 11/11/2026

Joe Blundy, Marcus Kennington and Chris Lailey met with Nick Cox and Mike Smith of WCCL at the clubhouse at 10.30am. The meeting ended at 1pm.

There were two parts to the meeting:

  1. Showing the WCCL representatives the facilities and answering the items on their checklist.
  2. Expressing our thoughts and feelings about the denial of promotion because of the Pitch Mark System.

Part One

NC explained that the subcommittee had been formed in response to a number of needs:

  1. Changes to the safeguarding environment that the ECB were going to implement.
  2. The fact that the number of teams in the WCCL has been growing rapidly.
  3. The Pitch Mark System has created controversy two years in a row and, with climate change, is likely to remain a problem.

NC went on to say that the subcommittee would visit every club in the WCCL both to build a better database and, perhaps more importantly, to improve communication between clubs and the committee.

Their aims are:

  1. To help clubs identify gaps in their safeguarding, facilities and ground management and suggest how to close those gaps.
  2. To help clubs in Divisions 1 and 2 align more closely with WEPL because it is a significant step up from a compliance perspective.
  3. To improve the Pitch Mark System and avoid the drama of the previous two years.
  4. To report at the AGM in 2027 with observations and proposals for structural change.

We spent 45 minutes answering questions on their checklist, walking through the clubhouse and inspecting the square. This was the data-gathering exercise they intend to undertake at all clubs. They were generally positive about our setup, and JB and MK were able to give a very good account of our ground and pitch maintenance.

The visitors were impressed by the wicket, our preparation for winter and our actions and plans to improve the quality of the outfield, including spiking, raking and anti-rabbit measures.

Like any good cricket match, we then had tea and biscuits between innings.

Part Two

NC explained that GBCC had been prioritised for an early meeting because the subcommittee recognised that we had substantial grievances regarding our treatment at the end of the season. They hoped that listening to our views might provide some closure and influence recommendations to be made at the AGM in 2027.

This discussion lasted approximately 90 minutes. It is of interest from the perspective of cricket development in Wiltshire but contains nothing of material significance to our club for the season ahead.

CL asked NC to describe how the subcommittee hoped to improve the Pitch Mark System (PMS). He outlined a process that changes very little in principle: marks would continue to be awarded by the visiting captain using the same criteria and anonymity.

The subcommittee's role next season would be to:

  1. Review all weekend pitch marks each Monday.
  2. Look for week-on-week trends, particularly among teams competing for promotion.
  3. Communicate promptly with clubs whose promotion prospects may be affected.

NC argued that substantial change would take time and would have to wait until AGM 2027, by which point the committee would have a clearer understanding of the issues and potential solutions.

CL was then invited to explain how GBCC felt. He stated that the club was very angry because promotion had been denied in a manner that felt unfair, driven by three key issues:

  1. We were powerless in the process. There was no provision for appeal, and we were informed of the ruling three weeks after the season ended and only one week before AGM submission deadlines.
  2. The PMP is fundamentally unsafe because there is no accountability. Marks are awarded anonymously, without transparency, and those involved are never held accountable.
  3. The PMP is entirely subjective and depends on knowledgeable, impartial judgement. In practice, marks are often emotional judgements made without sufficient understanding of how pitches should be assessed.

We worked hard throughout the year to provide a good pitch that consistently delivered competitive cricket.

MS agreed with this assessment, citing a late-season match against Bradford-on-Avon and stating, "I didn't have a problem with the pitch. I thought it was a good wicket."

The argument was made that promotion had been lost not because the pitch was inadequate, but because the PMP, Rule 15 and the WCCL committee processes were inadequate.

The discussion continued with WCCL acknowledging the importance of timeliness and confirming that the subcommittee would communicate concerns more promptly in future. They reiterated that immediate solutions to all concerns were not possible.

CL stressed that transparency was essential for meaningful improvement and that it must be accompanied by education so that captains understand both how to mark pitches and the responsibilities associated with those assessments.

NC suggested establishing Pitch Mark Forums similar to umpire forums. MS agreed that this would be a useful step.

CL asked how the effectiveness of such forums would be measured. WCCL agreed this would be important but admitted that no method had yet been developed.

JB asked how communication about poor pitch marks late in the season would work in practice. It was suggested that a member of the Groundsmen's Association could visit affected clubs to assess conditions and provide practical advice.

CL maintained that such intervention would not have helped GBCC in 2026 because the issue lay with the marking system rather than the quality of the pitch.

The meeting concluded with NC reiterating that communication between clubs and WCCL was central to effective reform and that improvements would come incrementally.

Conclusion

The process remains very much a work in progress. The fact that the Chairman of the WCCL Committee dedicated a morning to meeting with us suggests that the committee may be more open to change than is sometimes perceived.

The proposed weekly review and communication process is positive because it demonstrates greater responsibility and may help prevent future injustices. However, it does not address the fundamental flaws in the Pitch Mark Process:

  • Lack of transparency.
  • Lack of accountability.
  • Lack of education around subjective judgement.

As a result, WCCL appears no closer to resolving these structural weaknesses.

After considerable time and reflection, it is now clear to me that Rule 15 should be abolished until a fair Pitch Mark Process exists. The quality of grounds in Wiltshire cricket is important and should be assessed, but promotion should not depend on the current flawed system.

It is also apparent that the WCCL committee has become entrenched in a paternalistic role that encourages excessive control and, in turn, promotes childish behaviour.

My recommendation is that GBCC continue to influence change by engaging with other clubs and preparing to make the 2027 WCCL AGM a productive and constructive meeting.
